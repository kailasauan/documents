Written Contributions by Kailash Union to the Special Rapporteur on Truth, Justice and Reparation on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights in the Context of Transitional JusticeUAN-REPORT-UNSR-TransitionalJustice-20250130-1
Submission · 30 January 2025 · Special Rapporteur on truth, justice and reparation · 5,561 words

The submission addresses economic, social and cultural rights within transitional justice frameworks, focusing on historical injustices against indigenous Hindu communities and persecution of KAILASA. The document documents a 2,000-year history described as the Hindu Holocaust, colonial-era intensification of rights violations, post-colonial state discrimination, and the establishment of KAILASA as a sovereign state. It argues for integrating economic, social and cultural rights into transitional justice mechanisms to restore dignity, cultural autonomy and equitable resource access to affected communities.

Submission to the Human Rights Council on Implementation of Human Rights-Based Approach in the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity FrameworkUAN-REPORT-UNHRC-BiodiversityHumanRights-20250000-1
Submission · 2025 · United Nations Human Rights Council · 8,476 words

This submission by Kailash Union responds to Human Rights Council Resolution 57/28, which requested a global analytical study on human rights-based approaches in the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework. The submission documents Kailash Union's integration of human rights in biodiversity strategies, traditional knowledge protection, monitoring mechanisms, and describes nature-based solutions rooted in Hindu ecological principles including organic agriculture, water stewardship, and protection of all life. The submission addresses challenges including allegations of persecution and calls for UN measures against certain entities.

Institutional Arrangements of States that Enable Minority Rights to FlourishUAN-REPORT-UNSR-MinorityRights-20240510-1
Submission · 10 May 2024 · Special Rapporteur on minority issues · 12,883 words

The submission by Kailash Union addresses institutional arrangements that enable minority rights to flourish. The document argues that colonial census operations and classification systems artificially categorized diverse communities, leading to systemic marginalization and human rights violations. It documents alleged violations of religious freedom, freedom from torture, liberty and security, and freedom of expression affecting minority communities globally, and describes the historical persecution of Hindu civilization.
