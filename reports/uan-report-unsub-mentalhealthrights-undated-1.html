A. Introduction

↑ Contents

Mental health is intricately connected to all Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), particularly SDG-3, which focuses on ensuring healthy lives and promoting well-being for all. To effectively address psychosocial disabilities, it is essential to first examine the root causes of mental health issues and the associated human rights challenges. The root cause of mental health issues often lies in the psychological, emotional, and physical aspects of an individual. Many of these issues stem from suppressed emotions, incompletions[1] from the past, and distorted beliefs about oneself. When individuals perceive themselves through the lens of struggle or inadequacy, they manifest those beliefs in their lives.

Powerful cognitions are the antidote to these debilitating thought patterns. By cultivating powerful cognitions—positive beliefs and affirmations about oneself—individuals can rewire their thought processes. This involves recognizing one's inherent divinity and potential, embracing self-love, and allowing oneself to manifest one’s full potential. As the Supreme Pontiff of Hinduism (SPH) Bhagavan Nithyananda Paramashivam revealed, “We are not human beings striving for a spiritual experience. We are spiritual beings enjoying a human experience.”

Additionally, a variety of factors such as nutritional deficiencies, imbalances in the endocrine system, and social environments can significantly contribute to mental health challenges. Engaging in practices like yoga, meditation, and self-reflection can help heal these deeper issues and lead to a more balanced state of being. By addressing these interconnected factors holistically, rather than merely focusing on surface-level symptoms, we can create a more supportive environment that promotes overall health and resilience in individuals facing mental health challenges.
