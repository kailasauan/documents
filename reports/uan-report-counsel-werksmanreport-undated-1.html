1.The Applicable Statute of Limitations Bars Prosecution of Ms. Rao's May 2006 Claim

On September 28, 2016, the state of California passed S.B. 813, effectively eliminating the statute of limitations for certain sex-based offenses, including Rape in violation of Penal Code section 261. (Cal. S.B. 813.) This law took effect on January 1, 2017, and applies to "crimes that were committed on or after January 1, 2017, and to crimes for which the statute of limitations that was in effect prior to January 1, 2017, has not run as of January 1, 2017." (Id.) Prior to this change in the law, the statute of limitations in effect limited prosecutions for certain enumerated sex offenses, including rape, to within 10 years of the "commission ofthe offense." (Id.; Pen. Code§ 801.1.) Thus, considering the statute of limitations in effect previously, the relevant inquiry for the purposes of S.B. 813 is whether the incidents alleged in the instant case took place 10 or more years before January 1, 2017.

Here, Ms. Rao describes three main incidents occurring within the state of California, which occurred over the course of several years. The first incident occurred in May 2006; the second in May/June 2007; and the third in May 2009. Thus, the May 2006 incident, in which Ms. Rao alleges she performed a strip tease for Mr. Nithyananda, is barred by the statute of limitations because it occurred more than 10 years prior to

2.Ms. Rao Alleges only Consensual Sexual Acts, Which Are Legal in the State of California

All of the enumerated sex offenses that could conceivably be applicable to Ms. Rao's allegations require a showing that she did not consent to the acts at issue. (See People v. Key (1984) 153 Cal.App.3d 868, 897 ['"[L]ack of consent' is ... an element of the corpus delicti which the prosecution must prove in its case-in-chief beyond reasonable doubt."].) California law defines "consent" as "'positive cooperation in act or attitude pursuant to an exercise of free will. The person must act freely and voluntarily and have knowledge of the nature of the act or transaction involved." (People v. Ogunmola (1987) 193 Cal.App.3d 274, 280.)

Significantly, California case law makes clear that consent is not vitiated by a mere showing of misrepresentation or even "fraud in the inducement" of sexual acts. In fact, the "'basic common law rule (is) that, unless there is statutory language to the contrary, whenever lack of consent is a necessary element of a crime, the fact that consent is obtained throughout misrepresentation will not supply the essential element of nonconsent."' (People v. Harris (1979) 93 Cal.App.3d 103, 114, quoting People v. Cook (1964) 228 Cal.App.2d 716, 719; see also People v. Stuedemann (2007) 156 Cal.App.4th 1, 7-8.) Thus, "when a victim consents to the defendant's act with full knowledge of the essential characteristics of the act, a conviction under the unconscious-due-to-fraud-in- fact concept cannot stand even though the victim was induced to consent by fraudulent representations as to the benefits resulting from the act." (People v. Stuedemann, supra, 156 Cal.App.4th at pp. 7-8.) Consequently, consent that is obtained through misrepresentation of the "benefits" of a sex act, is still proper consent under the law as long as both participants understood the essential nature and characteristics of the act.

Here, Ms. Rao's statements clearly demonstrate that she provided contemporaneous consent for all sexual acts that allegedly took place between herself and

8 Mr. Nithyananda in the State of California. Ms. Rao describes that she maintained a continuous sexual relationship with Mr. Nithyananda between February 2005 and December 2009. Thus, at the time of her first trip to California, she had already been engaged in this relationship with Mr. Nithyananda for over one year. There was no indication that she did not consent to sexual intercourse during the three trips to California. Moreover, Ms. Rao fails to describe any indication of reluctance to engage in sexual intercourse, or other resistance on her part. While she describes objecting, at the first trip, to performing a "strip tease" for Mr. Nithyananda, she does not allege voicing any objection to the actual sex acts.

In light of the significant evidence of her continuous consent at all relevant times, Ms. Rao's allegations against Mr. Nithyananda center around the idea that in the weeks and months after leaving the Nithyananda organization, she came to the realization that she had been "brainwashed" by Mr. Nithyananda's spiritual teachings and that she was being abused "without [}realizing" it. (Chargesheet, pp. pp. 5, 10.) Ms. Rao maintains that she provided contemporaneous consent for the sexual acts which occurred between herself and Mr. Nithyananda over the course of four years, between February 2005 and December 2009, but that this consent was procured through Mr. Nithyananda's misrepresentation as to the spiritual benefits arising from these acts. Thus, nowhere does Ms. Rao allege that she did not know the nature of her sexual actions with Mr. Nithyananda. In fact, Ms. Rao acknowledges that after her first sexual encounter with Mr. Nithyananda, she was "extremely guilty about losing my morals, cheating on my husband and having sex outside marriage." (Id. at p. 9.) Therefore, she acknowledged that she understood the nature of the sexual acts between herself and Mr. Nithyananda. Nonetheless, her sole contention is that Mr. Nithyananda misrepresented "the benefits resulting from the act," by leading Ms. Rao to believe that she "was progressing spiritually as Nithyananda's divine consort." (Id. at p. 10; People v. Stuedemann, supra, 156 Cal.App.4th at pp. 7-8.) As set forth in the cases cited above, any fraud or misrepresentation by Mr. Nithyananda in the inducement of sexual acts does not vitiate Ms. Rao's underlying consent for the acts. Thus, even assuming Ms. Rao's factual

9 allegations to be true, the sexual acts engaged in by Mr. Nithyananda and Ms. Rao were entirely legal in the State of California.

3.Ms. Rao's Allegations Do Not Constitute Rape in Violation of Penal Code Section 261

California pattern jury instructions provide that to "prove that the defendant is guilty of this crime, the People must prove" the following elements: "1. The defendant had sexual intercourse with a woman; 2. He and the woman were not married to each other at the time of the intercourse; 3. The woman did not consent to the intercourse; AND" either the victim "at the time [was] unconscious of the nature of the act, and this is known to the accused;"5 or "[t]he defendant accomplished the intercourse" through "force, violence, duress, menace, or fear of immediate bodily injury;" future threats of bodily harm; or a threat of official action." (CALCRIM 1000; Pen. Code § 261.)

Although she alleges that Mr. Nithyananda once "slapped" her, and once motioned as if to slap her, this does not amount to a physical threat or coercion. 4.Ms. Rao's Allegations Do Not Constitute Unlawful Sexual Intercourse Procured by False or Fraudulent Representation in Violation of Penal Code Section 266c

Penal Code section 266c, which prohibits unlawful sexual intercourse "procured by false or fraudulent representation with intent to create fear" similarly does not criminalize the behavior described by Ms. Rao. (See Pen. Code§ 266c.) The primary showing required to establish this crime is that the defendant "induce[d] any other person to engage in sexual intercourse, sexual penetration, oral copulation, or sodomy when his or her consent is procured by false or fraudulent representation or pretense that is made with the intent to create fear, and which does induce fear, and that would cause a reasonable person in like circumstances to act contrary to the person's free will, and does cause the victim to so act." (Id.) Thus, unlike Penal Code section 261 where "fraud in the fact" is sufficient to vitiate consent while fraud in the inducement is insufficient to
